# Nail'd It Spa — Customer Voice Research
*Built June 13, 2026. Forum foraging + review mining (the "Halo strategy" from the Sabri Suby masterclass). Goal: the exact words nail clients use, and what they are starving for, so offers and copy strike like lightning.*

## In one line
Women are exhausted from salon hopping. They want one place (one tech) that is gentle, listens, stays clean, and lasts, so they can finally stop searching.

## What they are starving for (ranked)
1. A tech who actually listens and does what they asked.
2. Gentle hands. No cuts, no bleeding, no pain.
3. Nails that last a real two to three weeks, not three days.
4. A salon they can see is clean.
5. Honest pricing with zero surprise upcharges.
6. To never be rushed or treated like a number.
7. The big one underneath it all: to find "my person" and stop salon hopping forever.

---

## The themes, with their real words and how to use each

### 1. Being rushed / treated like a number — confidence: HIGH
The most common complaint by far. The flip side is the most common praise, and Nail'd It already earns it in its own 5-star reviews.

Their words (verbatim):
- "I felt like she was behind on her day and trying to rush through me."
- "I just became a number to her... she admitted she didn't know my real name."
- "the average experience at a salon is rushed, awkward, and really just the bare minimum effort."
- Nail'd It praise: "worked with coming in a little towards closing with no sense of a rushed experience."

The angle it unlocks: "We never rush you." Position slowness as care. This is a clean, ownable lane.

### 2. Gentle hands (the fear of being hurt) — confidence: HIGH
A visceral fear, and Nail'd It already gets praised for the exact opposite.

Their words:
- "the nail tech cut my finger DEEP and didn't even offer an apology... continued filing my skin until I had to stop her and say it's bleeding."
- "every salon I went to they cut my cuticles and drew blood."
- Nail'd It praise: "Twee was very sweet and gentle... I was satisfied with how clean and painless my cuticles were handled."

The angle it unlocks: "Gentle hands" as a promise. Huge, because the fear is physical and they already deliver the cure.

### 3. Listen to me / match the photo — confidence: HIGH
Clients bring an inspo photo and get something else, then get talked over when they speak up.

Their words:
- "You say you don't like something and they explain it away so hard you start questioning your own brain."
- "she pretended to not see a difference."
- Praise (a rival): "asks for my input during every step to make sure my nails are exactly how I want them."

The angle it unlocks: "Show us the photo. We will match it." Listening as the headline promise.

### 4. Nails that actually last — confidence: HIGH
Lifting and chipping within days is rampant, and the standard they hold is clear.

Their words:
- "After just 4 days ALL of the nails were lifting at the base."
- "Your nails should last at least two weeks before any lifting or chipping."
- Nail'd It praise: "my acrylics last for 3-4 weeks (which I'm grateful for because I'm busy)."

The angle it unlocks: claim the lasting power they already deliver (3 to 4 weeks). Pair with a fix-it guarantee (see offer section).

### 5. No surprise upcharges — confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH
Hidden fees and bait and switch pricing burn trust fast.

Their words:
- "hidden fees... Sounds too much like bait and switch to me."
- "the price went up to $75... The math isn't mathing."
- "$10 just to change my nail shape (with no warning)."

The angle it unlocks: honest, no surprise pricing (already a stated brand value). Say it out loud as a differentiator.

### 6. A salon you can see is clean — confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH
Cleanliness is half comfort, half fear of infection. OC women literally search "clean nail salon."

Their words:
- "This place was dark, dingy, and dirty. All the pedi seats were covered in dust."
- "they reuse dirty towels AND buffers."
- Praise: "Probably the cleanest nail salon I've been to."

The angle it unlocks: show the clean station, sealed tools, fresh setup. Clean is a feature worth filming.

### 7. The dream: find "my person," stop salon hopping — confidence: HIGH
This is the emotional core. Loyalty attaches to a PERSON, not a logo. That is why Twee, Nancy, and Mindy matter.

Their words:
- "I ran thru just about every salon within a 5 mile radius hoping I'd finally get better results and then just gave up."
- "I even moved far away but I refuse to go anywhere else."
- "she knows me well... it basically was a therapy session."

The angle it unlocks: make the techs the heroes (within the no team spotlight rule, this is the artist credit done as warmth, not a team post). The promise: a tech who remembers you.

## How to turn this into an offer (the Sabri play)
- The marketing writes itself: every common horror story has a Nail'd It 5-star review that is its exact cure. Take one fear per post (rushed, cut, lifted, dirty, ignored) and answer it with a real promise. This is the "name the pain, then be the answer" play.
- Power guarantee: address the no accountability pain head on. Rivals laugh you out or charge 5 dollars to fix a nail that lifted in days. Nail'd It can say: "If a nail lifts or chips in the first week, we fix it free, no attitude." That one line beats most of the competition outright.
- Godfather first visit offer: a low risk first appointment to crack the salon hopping habit (a new client mani or pedi deal), built to win on the rebook, not the first sale. Lose a little up front, win on the client who never leaves.
- NEWIF hook: "Tired of salon hopping?" speaks straight to the meta desire. Or the gentle angle: "When was the last time a manicure didn't hurt?"
- Free value lead magnet idea: a short honest guide, "How to spot a clean, gentle nail salon (5 things to check before you sit down)." Positions Nail'd It as the honest expert and reaches women who are not booking this minute.
